However far apart, cities are tightly linked by social, economic and cultural ties. The flows between them — what we call globalisation — remove physical, social, cultural and economic barriers, and in doing so create competition between cities.

To stand out, cities need attractive, prestigious, iconic urban and architectural places with a clear identity. Such places raise the quality of the urban environment and are among the strongest factors in attracting capital and talent. Skyscrapers are one architectural expression of this competition.

> The purpose of architecture is to achieve the most beautiful, most liveable and most sensitive design — one that meets users’ needs to the fullest — within the framework of sustainability.

That is why architects bear first-degree responsibility to the city and its people. Tall buildings must be considered not just at the scale of the building but at the scale of the city: zoning rules that allow height must also consider transport and infrastructure. Collaboration between local government, designers, private and public developers, civil society and citizens is the right approach.

The success of a tall building should be measured not only by its commercial identity but by the quality of the public space it gives back to the city. That requires an interdisciplinary team — architects, engineers, planners, real estate and communications advisers — with the architect leading as the integrating force.

You may not be able to name every tower in a city, but hear the name of one and you will instantly know which city it is in.

— Alpay Demirci, Hürriyet, October 2013
